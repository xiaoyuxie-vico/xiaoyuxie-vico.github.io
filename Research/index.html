Research

Many scientific and engineering systems are governed by physical principles that purely data-driven models ignore, while first-principles simulation is often too slow or incomplete to be used directly. My research integrates physics and prior knowledge into machine learning (dimensional invariance, governing-equation structure, domain-specific features) to build AI that is interpretable, data-efficient, and scalable, and that works with the noisy, scarce measurements found in practice.

The work falls into three connected directions, with applications in materials and manufacturing, fluid mechanics, and large-scale ML systems.

01

Data-Driven Discovery of Governing Laws

Discovering interpretable physical laws (dimensionless numbers, scaling laws, and governing PDEs) from scarce, noisy measurements by building physical principles into the learning algorithm.

NewUNIDE: Cross-PDE Learning for Noise-Robust Discovery of Governing Equations
Xie, X. · arXiv:2611.20678 (2026)

Instead of fitting each equation from scratch, UNIDE learns across PDE families: a self-supervised denoiser, a shared encoder that predicts which candidate terms are active, and weak-form least squares with residual-based pruning. It outperforms SINDy, WSINDy, and PySR at every noise level from 1% to 80%.

02

Data-Driven Reduced-Order Modeling

Fast, accurate surrogates for spatiotemporal systems whose governing equations are unknown or too expensive to solve, by learning compact latent dynamics.

Smooth and sparse latent dynamics in operator learning with jerk regularization
Xie, X., Mowlavi, S., & Benosman, M.
NeurIPS 2025 Workshop ML4PS · arXiv:2402.15636

Jerk regularization on an implicit-neural-representation autoencoder with neural-ODE latent dynamics yields smoother, sparser latent trajectories, better accuracy and convergence, and inference at any spatial or temporal resolution. Demonstrated on unsteady Navier–Stokes flow.

arXiv
03

Machine Learning-Based Digital Twin

Predicting process–structure–property relationships in metal additive manufacturing from thermal histories, with domain knowledge built into the model.

Mechanistic data-driven prediction of as-built mechanical properties in metal additive manufacturing
Xie, X., Bennett, J., Saha, S., Lu, Y., Cao, J., Liu, W. K., & Gan, Z.
npj Computational Materials, 7(1), 1–12 (2021)

Wavelet transforms and convolutional neural networks map thermal histories to location-dependent mechanical properties, and importance analysis reveals the dominant temperature ranges and frequencies, using only a small amount of noisy experimental data.
